Clinical Standards

Understanding BMI Classifications

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a screening metric established by the World Health Organization (WHO) and public health organizations worldwide to assess weight-to-height categories across adult populations.

Classification Standard WHO Asian-Indian Standard* Health Risk
Underweight < 18.5 < 18.5 Nutritional Risk
Normal / Healthy 18.5 – 24.9 18.5 – 22.9 Lowest Risk
Overweight 25.0 – 29.9 23.0 – 24.9 Moderate Risk
Obesity Class I 30.0 – 34.9 25.0 – 29.9 Elevated Risk
Obesity Class II/III ≥ 35.0 ≥ 30.0 High Risk
*Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) & WHO Asian guidelines adjust cutoffs due to higher predisposition to visceral adiposity and insulin resistance at lower BMI levels.

Why BMI Is A Starting Point, Not The Entire Story

At Habit Fix Wellness, Coach Raju Raut always reminds clients that a single number on a scale or calculator never defines your overall health vitality:

  • Muscle vs. Fat Composition: BMI cannot differentiate between muscle mass, water retention, and adipose tissue. An athlete may register as "overweight" while carrying optimal lean muscle.
  • Visceral vs. Subcutaneous Fat: Where your body stores fat matters more than total weight. Fat around vital organs poses higher metabolic risks than subcutaneous fat.
  • Metabolic Markers & Daily Stamina: Blood pressure, resting heart rate, sleep quality, and daily energy are often far more indicative of true well-being than BMI alone.
